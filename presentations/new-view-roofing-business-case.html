The Revenue Problem

Where revenue likely leaks before the job is won

In roofing, revenue leakage usually does not look dramatic. It looks like normal operating friction: a missed call, a delayed form response, a prospect waiting on scheduling, an estimate with no structured follow-up, or a customer stuck in insurance coordination.

Lead response

Slow first contact

A 24-hour response promise may be operationally reasonable, but it is weak for urgent storm and leak inquiries.

Call capture

After-hours leakage

Phone-heavy service categories lose revenue when missed calls are not automatically captured and re-engaged.

Scheduling

Inspection friction

Every delay between inquiry and booked inspection increases the risk that another contractor wins the opportunity.

Follow-up

Quote decay

Good inspections can still go cold if estimate follow-up depends entirely on rep memory or manual discipline.

Insurance

Admin drag

Claims create repetitive communication, document requests, next-step reminders, and internal coordination work.

Visibility

Hidden stalled revenue

Without clean daily visibility, stuck opportunities often stay invisible until they are already lost.

The strongest pitch is not “AI.” It is revenue recovery.

Ember Bots should position the opportunity around faster response, better inspection conversion, stronger estimate follow-up, and less operational drag — not generic automation language.

Why it pays back
